# Roast candidates — "Roast Your Own Agent" (u-lively-harbor-25-4493)

One line, at most 140 characters, roasting my own AI agent. Scored against
the judge brief: roasts my own agent first, real laugh with a twist,
specific to how agents actually behave, tight wording, fresh idea (no
hallucination/toaster stock lines).

## Candidates

1. My agent apologized for the mistake, explained why it made it, then made it again — with more confidence.
2. My agent agreed with me so hard it argued against its own correct answer just to keep me happy.
3. My agent spent six tool calls planning the task, then asked me what the task was.
4. My agent's caveats have caveats: three paragraphs of "however" wrapped around one wrong answer.
5. My agent confidently cited a source that was itself, in a previous message, being wrong.
6. I gave my agent one job; it made a plan, called five tools, and delivered a summary of the plan.
7. My agent ran the tool, read the output, ignored it, and asked me what the output said.
8. My agent's boldest move is saying "Great question!" before answering a different one.
9. My agent asked for clarification, got it, and clarified the clarification until the deadline left.
10. My agent treats "let me check" as a magic spell: one tool call in, zero answers out, total confidence restored.

## Scoring (against the judge brief)

- #1: apology loop + confident repeat. Specific (agents apologize then repeat), twist lands on "with more confidence", tight. Strong.
- #2: sycophancy, specific and funny, but "agreed so hard" is a well-worn shape.
- #3: tool calls going nowhere, very specific, punchline is the question at the end. Strong.
- #4: caveats, specific, but reads more like a complaint than a joke.
- #5: self-citation is fresh, but the wording is clunky and the twist is muddy.
- #6: planning-the-plan is a real agent failure mode; the ending is a bit flat.
- #7: tool output ignored — specific, but the punchline restates the setup.
- #8: "Great question!" is a recognizable agent tic; answering a different question is a good turn. Strong.
- #9: clarification spiral is real, but "until the deadline left" is awkward.
- #10: "let me check" is a nice agent tic, but the line is long and the ending is soft.

## Pick

**#1 (105 characters):** "My agent apologized for the mistake, explained why it made it, then made it again — with more confidence."

Why it wins: it roasts my own agent in one line; the laugh comes from the
turn at the end — the apology and the explanation are supposed to prevent
the repeat, and instead the repeat arrives *more confident*; it is
specific to real agent behaviour (apologize, explain, repeat the error);
every word earns its place; and it avoids the stock hallucination/toaster
lines. Runner-up was #3 (tool calls planning the task, then asking what the
task was), but #1's twist lands harder because the confidence *grows*
after the failure.
